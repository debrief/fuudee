# Serial 4: a target on one course, for TMA practice

The sample exercise has a fourth serial. In serials 1 to 3 RED ONE changes course every hour or so, which is realistic but hard to learn TMA on: a long period of bearings never fits one course and speed, and **Suggest** rightly declines it. In serial 4 RED ONE holds one course and speed for the whole four hours, and BLUE ONE zigs about it so the bearings give range.

<!-- more -->

*The tracks and bearings are invented, like the rest of the exercise; the background traffic is real, anonymised open AIS.*

## What it is

- **When:** the exercise's second day, 16 June 2023, 08:00Z to 12:00Z.
- **RED ONE:** 240° at 5 knots, 20 m, throughout.
- **BLUE ONE:** zigs every half hour, between 200° and 280° at 6.5 knots, so on average it keeps pace with RED ONE, 2.5 to 4.5 nmi off it.
- **Sensors:** the Bow sonar holds RED ONE almost the whole serial (334 cuts); the towed array gives 118 ambiguous cuts, as in the other serials.
- **Background traffic:** seven vessels from that day's AIS, reduced from NOAA's archive as the first day's was. The first day's extract is untouched, so serials 1 to 3 are exactly as they were.

## Trying Suggest on it

Open serial 4, select the Bow sonar's cuts for the whole serial, choose **TMA solution from cuts…** and press **Suggest**. It fills 2.19 nmi (± 0.50 nmi), 241.7° and 5.0 kn; RED ONE was 2.51 nmi off at the first cut, on 240° at 5.0 kn. One cut is named as far off the fit. The towed array's cuts are refused until you keep a side.
