# Labels, symbols and arrows

How an analyst chooses how often a track is labelled with the time, marked with a symbol and given a direction arrow; where the map draws them, and how it keeps them legible at any zoom.

## Requirements cited

| SRD | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| CAP-32 | Select one or more tracks and set how often their fixes are labelled, marked with a symbol and given a direction arrow, each from 1 second to 24 hours; one change applies to every selected track; the intervals are held per track. |
| SUB-22 | Presentation content, like colour: each change is one recorded, undoable step. |
| SUB-01 | Times are UTC; the analyst's time zone is for display only. |
| SUB-03 | Positions between fixes are along the geodesic. |
| EXT-14 | A legacy plot file's content comes in, and what does not is listed. |
| NFR-13 | Everything works from the keyboard, and by touch. |

## Model

1. **Three intervals, held by the track.** Each track has `label_interval`, `symbol_interval` and `arrow_interval` on its own properties: one value for the track, never one per fix (product owner, 15 Oct 2026). Each is a duration, a whole number of seconds from 1 s to 24 h; or **off**; for labels and symbols, **all**, at every recorded fix, legacy Debrief's *All* (product owner, 15 Oct 2026; arrows have no *All*); or left out, Debrief's default. The defaults are what the map drew before: no time labels, no symbols, and a few arrows along the track's part in the time window (up to five, at the middle of legs). Arrows set **off** draw none. Schema 0.0.20.
2. **One step for all the tracks chosen.** Select the tracks (Ctrl-click in the outline or on the map), then **Frequencies: labels, symbols and arrows…** (the outline's menu, the Command Palette) or **Frequencies…** under *Track* in the playground. One change is one step for every track chosen, named for what it set: *Labels every 10 min, arrows every 5 min on BLUE ONE and RED ONE*; *Symbols off on 3 tracks*. Undo takes it back for all of them. The intervals stay per track, so tracks set together may later differ.
3. **The form.** A heading, the tracks it is for, and a chooser each for **Labels**, **Symbols** and **Arrows**, nothing else (the product owner rejects cluttered forms). Each shows what the tracks have now. Where they differ it shows **Mixed (unchanged)**, and a chooser left so changes nothing for any of them. The choices are Off (and, for arrows, **A few (default)**; for labels and symbols, **All (every fix)**), then every 1 s, 5 s, 10 s, 30 s, 1 min, 2 min, 5 min, 10 min, 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h and 24 h, legacy Debrief's choices, and **Other…**, a box to type an interval in: `90 s`, `1h30`, `2 hours`, `1 day`, or `all` (not for arrows). Beneath it the form says what it read (*Means every 1 h 30 min*) or what is wrong (*Say the unit, such as 30 s, 10 min or 1 h 30 min.*; *An interval is from 1 s to 24 h.*), and sends nothing until it is right. In VS Code the same choices come as three short lists, one after the other, each starting at what the tracks have now; typing an interval offers it at the top. Only what changed is sent: a chooser showing what every track already has changes nothing, and with nothing changed there is no step.
4. **On whole multiples of the interval, as legacy Debrief.** Items fall at each whole multiple of the interval of UTC: every 10 min is 0900, 0910, 0920, wherever the track starts (legacy `LightweightTrackWrapper.setFixes` rounds the track's start up to a multiple of the frequency). UTC has no daylight saving, so every 24 h is midnight Zulu every day. A track too short to hold a multiple gets one, at its start, as legacy Debrief labels it. Only within the time window and the track's span: outside the window a track is context (thin, faint or hidden as the plot says) and carries none, as it carries no arrows or dots.
5. **Where the track was then.** Between fixes, the position is along the geodesic in proportion to time, exactly as the square at current time is placed (`pointAt` in `geodesic.ts`, the same code as `positionAt`, held to `schemas/vectors/track-positions.json`). Legacy Debrief instead marked the first fix at or after each multiple, so with sparse fixes its labels said the fix's own time (0931); Debrief labels the time asked for, on the line. Nothing is drawn inside a gap in the data (fixes more than ten times the track's median time between fixes apart, as smoothing treats a gap): no position is made up where nothing was recorded.
6. **What is drawn.**
    - A **label** is the time, `HHmm`, legacy Debrief's default fix label; `HHmm.ss` where the interval is not whole minutes (or, at every fix, where the fix's time is not); and `ddHHmm` on the first label of each day that is drawn, so the day is never in doubt. It is in the analyst's time zone, Zulu by default, with no zone letter, as on a chart. It sits to port of the track's course, as legacy Debrief places a fix's label, at the end of a short leader in the track's colour, the text in the theme's text colour with a halo, so it is legible on any track colour and any theme. Labels never overlap: all tracks' labels are placed together, earliest first, each to port, or to starboard where port is taken (by another label, a track's name, a fix's own label or a track's end marker), or left out where both are; a fix's label always shown is placed first and never left out. They sit above arrows and markers, below the tracks' names.
    - A **symbol** is a small dot in the track's colour, with a thin halo in the map's colour.
    - An **arrow** is the map's chevron, centred on the line and pointing along the course there (the leg leaving a fix, at a fix; the last course it made, where the track stood still). None is drawn within 12 px of a track's first or last fix in the window, where an arrow on the end marker would read as a second arrowhead. A track with an arrow interval set (or off) draws none of the default few.
    - **At every fix** (all), each fix's time, until fixes would be closer on screen than the spacing; then the smallest preset at least the fixes' median time apart, thinned as any interval is.
7. **Thinned to fit, and said.** Zoomed out, items set close together would pile up. The map raises the interval to the smallest clock-friendly multiple of it that leaves them far enough apart on screen (labels 44 px, arrows 28 px, symbols 10 px, on the track's average speed across the screen), so thinned labels still fall on round times: 10 min becomes 20 min, 30 min or 1 h, never 50 min. Where the track is slower than its average, or doubles back, any item closer than that to the one before is left out. No track draws more than 100 labels, 150 arrows or 400 symbols, and only what is in view, or near it, is drawn, again after each pan or zoom. A quiet note above the key says what was thinned, naming the tracks where only some were: *Labels every 1 h, arrows every 30 min at this zoom; zoom in for more*; *Labels every 20 min on RED ONE at this zoom; zoom in for more*. It also says *some labels left out* where more than a quarter of a track's gave way to others.
    - **Cheap to pan.** Symbols and arrows are painted on one canvas, not drawn as thousands of elements; labels already drawn are kept from one pan to the next; each track is projected once, and positions are worked out once a time (along the straight line on the map's plane on legs under 2 km, where it is the geodesic to well under a millimetre; along the geodesic on longer ones). Budget, measured in the playground with 50 tracks of 2,000 fixes each, labels every minute, symbols every second and arrows every 30 s: redrawing them after a pan takes 5 to 35 ms, and a pan blocks the page once, for 50 to 90 ms, where it blocked it 10 to 14 times for over a second in all before (headless Chromium, software rendering). The budget: after a pan, within 50 ms to redraw and 100 ms blocked; zooming into the densest view, at most 300 ms, once. Zoomed in, every item comes back.
8. **A fix's own label or symbol, always (force on only).** A single fix can always show its time label, its symbol or both, whatever the track's intervals and at any zoom: `show_label` and `show_symbol` on the fix's mark (product owner, 15 Oct 2026). There is no per-fix *off*. A forced fix is drawn once where an interval falls on it too, and is never thinned away: it was asked for. Set from the fix's menu (right-click or press and hold the fix on the map; **Fixes…**): **Always show its time label**, **Always show its symbol** and their opposites in VS Code, **Time label** and **Symbol** buttons (pressed when on) in the playground's fix list; each change is one step (*BLUE ONE fix at 09:31:00Z: time label always shown*). The mark follows its fix through moves, splits, merges, smoothing and jump removal, as any mark does.
9. **Shown with the track's details.** The outline says each track's intervals: in VS Code, the track's tooltip ends *Labels every 10 min · Symbols off · Arrows every 5 min*; in the playground, a track with any set has a quiet second line naming those set, and the whole line when pointed at. A fix's details (its tooltip on the map, the fix list) say *time label always shown*, *symbol always shown*. Agents read the intervals in the plot `debrief/open` returns, on each track's properties.
10. **Agents.** `debrief/set_track_frequencies` with `features` (track ids) and any of `label_interval`, `symbol_interval`, `arrow_interval`: a duration (`{value, unit}`), seconds, text such as `"10 min"` or `"1h30"`, `"off"`, `"all"` (labels and symbols at every fix), or `null` for the default. One step; refused, saying why, for an interval outside 1 s to 24 h, arrows at every fix, or a feature that is not a track. `debrief/mark_fix` takes `show_label` and `show_symbol` (true, or false to stop).

  actor Analyst
  participant Form as Frequencies form

11. **Legacy plots.** A .dpf file's display rules (`CoreFormatter`: *Label*, *Symbol* or *Arrow*, every so many milliseconds, for one named track or every track) become those tracks' intervals; legacy *All* (an interval of 0) becomes `all` for labels and symbols. Legacy Debrief writes no frequency on a track itself, so where no rule says, the fixes it showed with their label, symbol or arrow give the interval, where they fall exactly as legacy Debrief would have shown them at a preset, or at every fix. Everything else is listed in the import notes: an inactive rule, an interval outside 1 s to 24 h, arrows at every fix, a rule naming no imported track, a symbology match (legacy did not apply it either), a rule not on regular intervals (applied on whole multiples here), and arrows shown at no interval (product owner, 15 Oct 2026). Labels and symbols shown at no interval are kept as those fixes always showing theirs, and the notes say how many (product owner, 16 Oct 2026).

## Keyboard and touch

The form is a form: `Tab` between choosers, arrow keys within one, `Enter` applies, `Escape` cancels. In VS Code the lists are VS Code's own. A fix's menu opens by right-click, press and hold on a touch screen, or the fix list from **Fixes…** ([keyboard equivalents](keyboard-equivalents.md)).

## Reference implementation

- **Model and Python:** `label_interval`, `symbol_interval`, `arrow_interval` (`FixIntervalOffEnum`, `FixIntervalAllEnum`) on `TrackProperties` and `show_label`, `show_symbol` on `FixMark` in `schemas/linkml/features.yaml`; the rules in `debrief_schemas.documents`; reading and naming intervals in `debrief_kernel.time` (`read_interval`, `describe_interval`), held with TypeScript to `schemas/vectors/fix-intervals.json`; `debrief_stac.frequencies` and `Catalogue.set_track_frequencies`; `debrief_stac.fixes.marked` for a fix's own; the service method `debrief/set_track_frequencies`; display rules and shown fixes in `debrief_io.dpf` and `debrief_io.dpf_import` (`_track_frequencies`).
- **Map and forms:** `vscode/webview/frequencies.ts` (the form's choices, times, thinning, labels, a track's intervals in words), `drawGrid` and `paintMarks` in `vscode/webview/map.ts`, `pointAt` in `vscode/webview/geodesic.ts`; the command `debrief.setTrackFrequencies` in `vscode/src/extension.ts`; the playground's form in `vscode/play/host.ts`.
- **Tests:** `python/debrief-stac/tests/test_frequencies.py`, `python/debrief-io/tests/test_dpf_frequencies.py`, `python/debrief-kernel/tests/test_intervals.py`, `vscode/test/unit/frequencies.test.ts`, `vscode/test/play/frequencies.mjs`, and a step of `vscode/test/integration/suite.ts`.
